# AL RIWAYAH — The Statement

Arabic title: **الرواية**  
Internal identifier: `al-riwayah`  
Product type: mobile-first local multiplayer party game + complete marketing website  
Gameplay devices: **players' phones only; no television required**  
Supported players: **4–6**  
Target session: **10–15 minutes**  
Primary language: **Arabic, RTL, Saudi-friendly conversational copy**  
Secondary language readiness: English architecture, optional later localization


## Purpose

Technical correctness does not prove that planning, contradiction reveal, and patching are fun. Human playtests must validate the emotional loop.

## Test waves

### PT-0 Paper/system simulation

4 players, no polished UI. Validate question logic and patch consequences.

Success:

- contradiction understood in under 10 seconds;
- players debate patch without facilitator;
- patch creates a follow-up players recognize as fair.

### PT-1 Playable review build

Two separate groups of 4–6.

At least one group should not know the designer well.

Observe without teaching beyond built-in onboarding.

### PT-2 Stress and replay

Same group plays twice with different evidence/question selection. Measure whether they use a rigid “safe template.”

## Metrics

- join time;
- rule comprehension time;
- “what do I do?” count;
- planning participation by player;
- number of spontaneous discussions;
- number of laughter/surprise moments;
- unintended silence duration;
- number of players who felt their private evidence mattered;
- contradiction comprehension;
- perceived fairness;
- patch decision time;
- desire to replay;
- remembered moment after 10 minutes;
- repeated safe strategy;
- phase ranked best/worst.

## Post-game questions (1–5 scale)

1. I understood what I needed to do.
2. My decisions affected the group.
3. The contradiction rules felt fair.
4. Patching was more interesting than simply changing an answer.
5. The result explained what happened.
6. I would play another case.
7. I felt included throughout.

Open:

- What exact moment do you remember?
- Which contradiction felt unfair, if any?
- Did you feel you were memorizing or improvising?
- Was there a safe strategy?
- Which phase should be shorter?
- What would make you ask for another round?

## Success criteria

The core loop passes when across at least two groups:

- ≥80% understand the contradiction without facilitator explanation;
- ≥70% rate patching 4 or 5;
- ≥70% want another case;
- every player performs meaningful private actions;
- average unintended silence under 15 seconds per phase;
- at least one remembered player-created moment per session;
- no single player authors over 70% of planning facts in both groups;
- result fairness average ≥3.8/5.

## Redesign triggers

- Players describe it mainly as a memory test.
- Contradiction feels arbitrary.
- Patch is obviously optimal.
- Players forget the new commitment immediately.
- Final report surprises players for reasons not shown.
- Six-player planning excludes quieter players.
- More than one phase repeatedly rated 1–2.

## Feedback processing

- Tag observations by phase and mechanic.
- Require repeated evidence or a severe blocker before rule changes.
- Separate preference from comprehension/fairness failure.
- Convert accepted findings into issue with reproduction, expected emotion, and measurable acceptance.
- Update `DECISIONS.md` and risk register.
